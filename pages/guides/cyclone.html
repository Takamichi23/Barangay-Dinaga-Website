What to do in case of a

Tropical Cyclone

A tropical cyclone is an extreme weather system characterized by large-scale circulation of strong winds, low atmospheric pressure, and heavy rainfall. It can trigger flooding, storm surges, landslides, and flash floods.

Before

  1. Monitor the news for weather updates, warnings, and advisories.
  2. Know the early warning signals and evacuation plan of your community.
  3. Check the structural integrity of your house and repair weak parts.
  4. Prepare your family’s Emergency Go Bag containing essential survival supplies.
  5. Move livestock and pets to a safe area or designated evacuation site for animals.
  6. When advised by authorities, immediately proceed to the designated evacuation center.

During

  1. Stay calm, remain indoors, and keep tuned in to the latest weather advisories.
  2. Turn off the main electrical switch and shut off the water valve if flooding threatens.
  3. Use flashlights or emergency lamps; exercise extreme caution when using candles or gas lamps.
  4. Stay away from glass windows and doors.

After

  1. Wait for authorities to officially declare that it is safe to return home.
  2. Stay clear of fallen trees, damaged structures, and downed power lines.
  3. Avoid sightseeing so emergency response crews can work unimpeded.
  4. Be cautious when inspecting and repairing damaged parts of your house.
  5. Check for wet or submerged electrical outlets and appliances before turning power back on.
  6. Empty rainwater from cans, pots, and old tires to prevent mosquito breeding.
