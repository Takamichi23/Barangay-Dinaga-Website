Tips to prevent

Leptospirosis

Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection transmitted primarily through floodwater or soil contaminated with the urine of infected animals, particularly rodents. Preventing infection relies on avoiding contaminated water and maintaining strong hygiene:

1. Avoid Wading in Floodwaters

  • Stay out of flooded areas: Leptospirosis is most commonly contracted through direct contact with floodwaters contaminated by animal urine.
  • Use protective gear: If passing through floodwater is unavoidable, wear waterproof boots and gloves—especially if you have cuts or open wounds.

2. Maintain Proper Sanitation

  • Control rodent populations: Dispose of food waste properly and keep home and workplace surroundings clean.
  • Seal food safely: Store food and drinking water in tightly covered, rodent-proof containers.
  • Fix leaks and drainage: Keep drains clear and repair plumbing leaks to prevent stagnant water that attracts pests.

3. Practice Good Hygiene

  • Wash thoroughly after exposure: Clean your hands, feet, and skin with soap and clean water immediately after any contact with floodwater, mud, or animals.
  • Protect open wounds: Clean and cover cuts, scratches, or abrasions with waterproof dressings.

4. Seek Prompt Medical Care

  • Wear protective gear in high-risk zones: Farmers, flood responders, and residents in flood-prone zones should consistently wear boots and gloves.
  • Consult a health worker early: If you were exposed to floodwater or experience fever, chills, muscle aches, or red eyes, visit the Barangay Health Center right away for evaluation and prophylaxis.
